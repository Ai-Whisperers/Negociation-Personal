# OBJECTION HANDLING CHEAT SHEET

**Purpose:** Quick responses to common objections during negotiation
**Use:** Keep this visible during the meeting, memorize the top 5

## TOP 10 OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES

### 1️⃣ "THAT'S TOO EXPENSIVE"

**What they really mean:** "I don't see the value yet" OR "I need to justify this to my boss"

**Response:**
```
"I understand it's an investment. Let me show you the ROI:

Building in-house would cost you $135,000 and 12 months.
Our platform costs [your price] and deploys in 2 months.

You're saving $130,000+ and 10 months of time.

If you analyze 10,000 comments/month manually, that's 40 hours
at $50/hour = $2,000/month = $24,000/year.

Our platform costs $32/month after the initial investment.

You'll have paid off the entire investment in [X] months, and
everything after that is pure profit.

Does that change the perspective?"
```

**Alternative (softer):**
```
"Compared to what? If you're comparing to doing nothing, yes,
it's an investment. But compared to:
- Manual analysis ($24k/year)
- Building in-house ($135k)
- Enterprise SaaS ($80k over 3 years)

We're 90-98% cheaper. What's your alternative?"
```

### 2️⃣ "WE CAN BUILD THIS IN-HOUSE"

**What they really mean:** "We have developers, why should we pay you?"

**Response:**
```
"Absolutely, your team is capable. The question isn't CAN you,
it's SHOULD you.

Here's what we learned building this:
- 300+ hours of development
- 15,000 lines of production code
- 4 microservices orchestrated
- Hybrid AI optimization (87% cost reduction)
- Deduplication algorithm (O(n) with fuzzy matching)
- Production-ready deployment

That's 2-3 months of full-time work for 3 developers.

PLUS ongoing maintenance: 20% of development time forever.

So the question is: Would you rather have your developers spend
300 hours building commodity infrastructure, or building features
that differentiate your product and generate revenue?

We're selling you 300 hours of work for [your price]. That's
[$X/hour - calculate based on price]. Can your team work for that?"
```

**Follow-up question:**
```
"What could your developers build in those 300 hours that would
generate MORE than [your price] in revenue or cost savings?

That's your opportunity cost."
```

### 3️⃣ "WE NEED TO THINK ABOUT IT"

**What they really mean:** "I'm not convinced yet" OR "I need to talk to others" OR "I'm avoiding saying no"

**Response (Discovery):**
```
"Of course, this is an important decision. To help you think it
through, can I ask:

1. What specific concerns do you have that I can address today?
2. What would need to be true for this to be a clear 'yes'?
3. Who else needs to be involved in the decision?
4. What's your timeline for deciding?"
```

**Response (Create Urgency - if appropriate):**
```
"I completely understand. Just so you know:

- Our current pricing is locked for [X] days (after that it may
  increase due to [reason: infrastructure costs, demand, etc.])
- We have [X] implementation slots available this quarter
- The sooner we start, the sooner you see ROI

What questions can I answer to help you decide?"
```

**Response (Close or Walk):**
```
"I appreciate that. Let me ask: On a scale of 1-10, how interested
are you in moving forward?

[If 7-10]: Great! What would it take to get to a 10?
[If 4-6]: What's holding you back from a higher number?
[If 1-3]: It sounds like this might not be the right fit right now.
          Is there a better time to revisit this?"
```

### 4️⃣ "WE DON'T HAVE THE BUDGET RIGHT NOW"

**What they really mean:** "This isn't a priority" OR "We need to shift budget"

**Response (Explore):**
```
"I understand budget constraints. Can I ask:

1. When does your next budget cycle open?
2. What budget category would this fall under?
   (IT? Operations? Customer Success? Product?)
3. If budget weren't an issue, would you want to move forward?

[If yes to #3]:
Let's talk about ways to make this work:
- Payment terms: 50% now, 30% in 30 days, 20% in 60 days
- Delayed start: Sign now, implement next quarter
- Smaller package: Start with Small, upgrade later
- ROI-based pricing: Pay us based on savings you achieve

What works best for you?"
```

**Alternative (Reframe):**
```
"I hear you. Let me ask: What's the cost of NOT solving this problem?

If you're losing customers to churn that you could have saved...
If you're building features nobody wants because you're guessing...
If your team is spending 40 hours/month on manual analysis...

What's that costing you right now?

Our platform costs [your price]. How many churned customers would
that save? How many wasted feature-hours would that prevent?

Often, the cost of inaction is higher than the cost of action."
```

### 5️⃣ "CAN YOU DO BETTER ON PRICE?"

**What they really mean:** "I'm interested but want a discount" (Test your flexibility)

**Response (Don't discount immediately):**
```
"I'd like to work with you on this. Before we talk about price,
let me understand:

1. If we adjust the price, what can you be flexible on?
   - Payment terms?
   - Timeline?
   - Package tier?
   - Testimonial/case study?
   - Referral to other companies?

2. What price did you have in mind?

[Let them anchor first - important!]

3. Is price the only thing preventing you from moving forward,
   or are there other concerns?

[If price is the only issue]:
Let me see what I can do. If we can find a number that works,
can you commit today?"
```

**Response (Conditional Discount):**
```
"Our standard pricing is [your price], which already includes
[X, Y, Z value]. That said, I can offer:

- 5% discount IF you commit this week
- 10% discount IF you prepay annually
- Extended support (60 → 90 days) IF you provide a case study
- Free customization IF you refer another client

Which of these works best for you?"
```

**Hard Pass Response (If they push below 15 units):**
```
"I appreciate your position, but at that price point, I wouldn't
be able to deliver the quality and support you deserve.

Our minimum is [15 units = X price]. Below that, it's not sustainable
for us to provide the service you need.

If budget is the concern, we can look at:
- Payment terms (spread over 3-6 months)
- Starting with a smaller package and upgrading later
- Delayed implementation (sign now, start next quarter)

But I can't go below [15 units] and still deliver value. Does that
make sense?"
```

## OBJECTION HANDLING FRAMEWORK

**When you hear an objection, follow this 4-step process:**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 4-STEP OBJECTION HANDLING                                  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. ACKNOWLEDGE                                              │
│    "I understand that concern. It's a valid question."     │
│                                                             │
│ 2. CLARIFY                                                  │
│    "Can you tell me more about [specific concern]?"        │
│    "What would need to be true to address that?"           │
│                                                             │
│ 3. RESPOND                                                  │
│    [Use responses from above]                              │
│                                                             │
│ 4. CONFIRM                                                  │
│    "Does that address your concern?"                       │
│    "What else is on your mind?"                            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**NEVER:**
- ❌ Get defensive ("That's not true!")
- ❌ Dismiss their concern ("That's not a real issue")
- ❌ Argue ("You're wrong about that")

**ALWAYS:**
- ✅ Acknowledge ("I hear you")
- ✅ Validate ("That's a fair concern")
- ✅ Reframe ("Here's another way to look at it")

## READING THE OBJECTION

**True objection vs. Smokescreen:**

```
TRUE OBJECTION:
→ Specific concern ("We're worried about data privacy")
→ Asks for details ("How does the AI work?")
→ Engaged body language
→ SOLUTION: Address it directly

SMOKESCREEN (Hiding real issue):
→ Vague concern ("It's too expensive")
→ Avoids specifics ("We need to think about it")
→ Checked-out body language
→ SOLUTION: Dig deeper with questions
```

**Questions to uncover real objection:**
- "What's your biggest concern about moving forward?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how interested are you? What would make it a 10?"
- "If [objection] weren't an issue, would you move forward?"

## WALK-AWAY SIGNALS

**Know when to stop negotiating:**

```
WALK AWAY IF:
❌ Price < 15 units (your hard minimum)
❌ Unreasonable demands ("Free lifetime support")
❌ No decision-maker present (wasting time)
❌ They're not the right fit (wrong use case)
❌ Red flags (late to meetings, rude, unrealistic)

HOW TO WALK AWAY GRACEFULLY:
"I appreciate your time, but I don't think we're the right fit
right now. If your situation changes, feel free to reach out.

In the meantime, I'd recommend [alternative solution that might
fit their budget/needs]."
```

**Maintain relationship:**
- Don't burn bridges
- Offer to stay in touch
- Suggest alternatives
- Leave door open

## FINAL TIPS

**Mental Preparation:**
1. **Expect objections** - They're a sign of interest, not rejection
2. **Stay calm** - Objections are questions, not attacks
3. **Listen first** - Understand the real concern before responding
4. **Practice** - Rehearse top 5 objections 3 times tonight

**During the Meeting:**
1. **Pause before responding** - Don't rush your answer
2. **Ask clarifying questions** - "Can you tell me more about that?"
3. **Use stories/examples** - "Here's how we handled that..."
4. **Check for understanding** - "Does that address your concern?"

**Win-Win Mentality:**
- Not adversarial (you vs. them)
- Collaborative (let's solve this together)
- Focus on value, not price
- Walk away if not the right fit
