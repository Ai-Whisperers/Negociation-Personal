## II. Anchoring Strategy

### The Reverse-Anchor Approach

**From Playbook Abstract:**
> "Reverse-anchor: ask their **first number**; internal **hard-pass <15**; work the **25–15** corridor."

**Primary Strategy: Let Them Go First**

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "Before we discuss pricing, I'd like to understand: what budget range were you considering for this solution?"

**Why This Works:**
1. ✅ They might anchor HIGHER than our target (80M PYG)
2. ✅ Shows we're confident in our value
3. ✅ We're not constrained by a low expectation
4. ✅ We learn their budget reality

### Response Matrix

**If They Anchor:**

| Their Number | Our Response | Strategy |
|--------------|--------------|----------|
| **≥100M PYG** | Accept + add value | "That works for us. Let me show you everything included at that level." |
| **80-100M PYG** | Accept | "Perfect. That aligns with our target package." |
| **60-80M PYG** | Work with it | "We can work in that range. Let me show you our Medium tier." |
| **40-60M PYG** | Counteroffer +Δ | "We were targeting 80M, but we could do 60M if we structure it as [pilot/payment terms/added value]." |
| **15-40M PYG** | Pilot or walk | "That's below our full package price. We could do a 10-day pilot at that level, or a limited tier. Would that interest you?" |
| **<15M PYG** | Hard pass | "I appreciate the offer, but that's below our minimum cost. For that budget, we could only offer [very limited scope]. Would you like to discuss a larger budget?" |

**If They Don't Give a Number:**

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "No problem. Let me show you our tier structure. We have Small, Medium, and Large packages with different token limits and support levels. The typical range is 40-80 million PYG depending on volume and features. Does that fit within your planning?"

**Then Present:**
1. Show tier structure (S/M/L)
2. Emphasize value at each level
3. Let them choose
4. Negotiate from their choice

## IV. Pricing Corridor Strategy

### The 25-15 Corridor

**From Playbook Abstract:**
> "Work the **25–15** corridor"

**Interpretation (in context of M PYG):**
- High anchor: 80-100M PYG (25% above target)
- Target: 60-80M PYG (sweet spot)
- Minimum: 40M PYG (15M above hard pass)
- Hard pass: <15M PYG (walk away)

**Negotiation Flow:**

```
100M ┤ Stretch goal
     │
 80M ┤ ← START HERE (target)
     │
 60M ┤ Medium tier (acceptable)
     │
 40M ┤ ← MINIMUM (floor)
     │
 15M ┤ ← HARD PASS (walk away below this)
     │
  0M └─────────────
```

**Concession Pattern:**
- Start: 80M PYG
- Concede to: 70M PYG (if they push)
- Concede to: 60M PYG (if they give something in return)
- Floor: 40M PYG (absolute minimum)

**NEVER go below 40M without:**
- Pilot model (limited scope)
- OR significant non-financial value (testimonial + referrals)
- OR payment in full upfront

## V. Negotiation Tactics

### Tactic 1: The ROI Bomb

**When to Use:** When they question price

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "I understand the concern about price. Let's look at the ROI. You're currently spending $24,000/year on manual analysis, plus the opportunity cost of missed churn signals and wrong product decisions - totaling about $364,000/year. Our solution costs $6,098 in Year 1, saving you $357,902. It pays for itself in less than a week. Would you like to see the detailed ROI calculator?"

**Why It Works:**
- Reframes from "cost" to "investment"
- Shows massive ROI (5,873%)
- Makes price seem tiny compared to problem cost
- Data-driven (hard to argue with numbers)

### Tactic 2: The Comparison Wedge

**When to Use:** When they mention alternatives

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "Let's compare your options:
> - Enterprise SaaS: $80,000 over 3 years, 3-6 months to deploy, vendor lock-in
> - Build in-house: $135,000, 12 months, your devs can't work on revenue features
> - Status quo: $24,000/year lost forever, customers churning
> - Our solution: $5,714, 2 weeks to deploy, source code included
>
> Which of these makes the most sense for Personal Paraguay?"

**Why It Works:**
- Side-by-side comparison shows we're the obvious choice
- Highlights our unique advantages (speed, source code, cost)
- Makes alternatives look expensive and slow

### Tactic 3: The Champion Support

**When to Use:** Throughout negotiation

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "José Luis, I know you need to get approval from your CFO, CTO, and executive team. We want to make that process as easy as possible for you. We'll provide:
> - Executive summary (1-pager for decision-makers)
> - ROI calculator (financial justification for CFO)
> - Technical specs (for CTO review)
> - Comparison matrix (vs. alternatives)
>
> Is there anything else that would help you present this internally?"

**Why It Works:**
- Positions us as partners, not vendors
- Makes José Luis the hero (we support his pitch)
- Reduces friction in approval process
- Builds relationship

### Tactic 4: The Quick Win Frame

**When to Use:** To create urgency

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "If we move forward this week, you could have automated insights running by November 1st. That means you can show results in your year-end review - automated NPS reports, churn risk analysis, data-driven product roadmap. That's a great career win. Plus, if you have budget available this fiscal year, you can deploy now instead of waiting for next year's budget cycle."

**Why It Works:**
- Appeals to José Luis's career interests
- Creates budget cycle urgency
- Shows fast time-to-value
- Personal benefit (not just company benefit)

### Tactic 5: The Concession Trade

**When to Use:** When they ask for discount

**From INTERNAL-TEAM-QUESTIONNAIRE:**
> "GOLDEN RULE: Never give without getting. Every concession should be traded for something of equal value."

**Script:**
> **Kyrian:** "I understand you'd like a discount. We're already priced 90-98% lower than enterprise alternatives, but I can offer you 60M instead of 80M if you can provide:
> - A video testimonial we can use on our website
> - Introduction to 2 other companies in your network who might benefit from this
> - A case study featuring Personal Paraguay
>
> Would that work?"

**Why It Works:**
- Never concedes price without getting value
- Testimonial/referrals are worth more than money (first client)
- Makes them think about what they're willing to give
- Often they'll accept the original price instead

## VI. Objection Handling

### Objection 1: "That's too expensive"

**Response A (ROI Focus):**
> "Compared to what? The $364,000/year you're currently losing to manual processes and missed opportunities? Or the $135,000 it would cost to build in-house? Our $5,714 solution pays for itself in less than a week."

**Response B (Pilot Option):**
> "I understand. Would a 10-day pilot make more sense? You can see the results with your own data for 20-30M PYG, and if it works, we'll credit that toward the full package."

### Objection 2: "You have no track record / We're taking a risk"

**Response:**
> "That's absolutely true - you'd be our first reference client. That's exactly why we're offering competitive pricing and source code included. Many companies would charge 10x more and lock you into their ecosystem. With us, you get a production-ready platform that's already been tested with 850 comments, plus zero vendor lock-in. And because you're first, we're highly motivated to make this a huge success - we need your testimonial and referral."

### Objection 3: "We can build this in-house"

**Response:**
> "You absolutely could. Let's compare:
> - **In-house:** $135,000 in development costs, 12 months of work, your developers can't work on revenue features during that time
> - **Our solution:** $5,714, deployed in 2 weeks, your team can focus on your core product
>
> What's the opportunity cost of 12 months for your product roadmap? And during that year, you'll lose another $364,000 to the current manual process. Total cost of building: $499,000. Or deploy with us in 2 weeks for $5,714."

### Objection 4: "We need to think about it / Can we get back to you?"

**Response:**
> "Of course, take the time you need. Can I ask - what specific concerns or questions do you still have? I want to make sure we've addressed everything so you can make an informed decision."

**Then:**
- Address their concerns
- Set specific follow-up date ("Can we reconnect on [date]?")
- Provide materials for internal discussion

### Objection 5: "Can we get a discount / What's your best price?"

**Response:**
> "We're already priced 90-98% lower than enterprise alternatives. We've built our pricing to be fair and sustainable. However, if you can provide a video testimonial and introduce us to 2 other companies, I could reduce the price to 60M instead of 80M. Would that work?"

**Alternative:**
> "Our pricing is based on value delivered - $357,902 in Year 1 savings. We're not looking to discount below our costs. What we can do is adjust the payment terms or structure it as a pilot. Which would be more helpful?"

## VIII. Black-Box Deployment Strategy

**From Playbook Abstract & Dirty Plays:**
> "Host it as **black-box SaaS**; no code handover."
> "Code/IP: SaaS license only, **no repo**, compiled build; logs + checksums."

### Why Black-Box?

**Protects Our IP:**
- They don't get source code until full payment
- Prevents them from copying and canceling
- Maintains our competitive advantage
- Protects our hybrid AI architecture innovation

### How It Works

**Deployment Model:**
1. **SaaS License** - They get access to platform, not code
2. **Compiled Build** - Deployed as compiled application
3. **No Repository Access** - No GitHub access during pilot/trial
4. **Logs + Checksums** - Prove ownership and track usage

**After Full Payment:**
- Source code included (as promised)
- They own their instance
- Zero vendor lock-in
- Full control

**Script:**
> "We deploy as a black-box SaaS initially to protect our IP during the pilot phase. Once you've paid in full, you get complete source code access and own your instance. This gives you zero vendor lock-in while protecting our technology during the evaluation phase. Does that make sense?"
