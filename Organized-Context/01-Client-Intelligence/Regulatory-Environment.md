## Data Protection & Privacy Regulations

### Critical Development: New Data Protection Law (2025)

**Timeline:**
- **May 27, 2025:** Chamber of Deputies approved Personal Data Protection Bill
- **June 5, 2025:** President signed and forwarded to Senate
- **Status:** Fully approved, awaiting publication
- **Effective Date:** 24 months after official publication (expected **2027**)

### Key Provisions of New Law

**Data Protection Principles:**
1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, Transparency**
2. **Purpose Limitation** - Data used only for stated purpose
3. **Data Minimization** - Collect only what's necessary
4. **Accuracy** - Keep data accurate and up-to-date
5. **Storage Limitation** - Delete when no longer needed
6. **Integrity and Confidentiality** - Secure data processing
7. **Accountability** - Organizations responsible for compliance

**Individual Rights:**
- ✅ **Access:** Right to know what data is held
- ✅ **Rectification:** Right to correct inaccurate data
- ✅ **Erasure:** Right to be forgotten
- ✅ **Opposition:** Right to object to processing
- ✅ **Data Portability:** Right to transfer data between services
- ✅ **Protection Against Automated Decision-Making:** Right to human review

**Regulatory Authority:**
- **New Entity:** Personal Data Protection Authority (independent)
- **Powers:** Monitor compliance, handle complaints, issue decisions, enforce sanctions
- **Oversight:** Full regulatory authority with enforcement capabilities

### Current Legal Framework (Pre-2027)

**Existing Laws (Limited):**
- **Law No. 1682/2001:** Regulates use of private information
- **Law No. 4868/2013:** Electronic Commerce regulations
- **Gap:** No comprehensive data protection framework currently
- **Status:** Scattered provisions across various sectors

**Current Situation:**
- ❌ No National Data Protection Authority exists yet
- ❌ No comprehensive enforcement mechanism
- ⏳ 24-month implementation window (2025-2027)

### International Alignment

**Standards:**
- **EU GDPR:** Data protection frameworks must align with GDPR principles
- **Council of Europe Convention 108+:** Reference standard
- **Regional Cooperation:** Following Latin American data protection trends

**Global AI Governance:**
Paraguay currently ranks **84th out of 138 countries** (score: 6.33/100) in AI governance, with **0 score in governmental frameworks component** due to absence of data protection law. The new law addresses this gap.

## Telecommunications Regulations

### Regulatory Authority: CONATEL

**Full Name:** Comisión Nacional de Telecomunicaciones de la República del Paraguay

**Role:**
- Regulator of national telecommunications system
- Verify product compliance with regulations
- Issue certificates for telecommunications equipment
- Manage spectrum allocation
- Oversee telecom service quality

**Certificate Requirements:**
- **Validity:** 5 years
- **Lead Time:** 11 weeks typical
- **Scope:** All wireless equipment operating in Paraguay

### Recent Regulatory Developments (2025)

#### 1. Wi-Fi 6E Authorization (April 2025)
- **Resolution:** No. 1035/2025 (April 23, 2025)
- **Authorized Band:** 5925-6425 MHz for low-power wireless access
- **Prohibited:** 6425-7125 MHz sub-band
- **Requirement:** Certificate of Approval from CONATEL
- **Impact:** Modernization of wireless infrastructure

#### 2. 5G Spectrum Allocation (January 2025)
- **Resolution:** No. 0148/2025 (January 22, 2025)
- **Amendment:** National Frequency Allocation Plan (PNAF)
- **Allocated Band:** 3300-3700 MHz for 5G fixed wireless access
- **Status:** No carriers assigned yet (auction completed separately)
- **Timeline:** 5G services launching March-April 2026

### Regulatory Environment Assessment

**Pro-Innovation Indicators:**
- ✅ **5G Spectrum:** Allocated and auctioned (demonstrating tech-forward approach)
- ✅ **Wi-Fi 6E:** Authorized in 2025 (keeping pace with global standards)
- ✅ **Data Protection:** New law aligns with international standards (GDPR-compatible)
- ✅ **National Plan:** Telecommunication Plan 2021-2025 promotes service expansion
- ✅ **AI Hub Strategy:** Government positioning Paraguay as regional AI leader

**Compliance Culture:**
- **Risk Level:** Medium - Regulations exist but enforcement is developing
- **Trend:** Moving toward stricter compliance (new data protection law)
- **Business Environment:** Government encouraging digital transformation

## Regulatory Due Diligence Questions to Expect

### Questions Personal May Ask

**Q1: "Does your solution comply with Paraguay's new data protection law?"**
**A:** Yes. Our architecture implements privacy-by-design principles aligned with the new law: data minimization (feedback text only), purpose limitation (analysis only), storage limitation (24-hour deletion), and transparency (full AI documentation). We're ready for 2027 enforcement.

**Q2: "Where is our data processed and stored?"**
**A:** Data is processed in the cloud (Render.com) with automatic 24-hour deletion. However, we can deploy locally on your infrastructure if data sovereignty is a requirement. You control where your data lives.

**Q3: "Do we need CONATEL approval for this solution?"**
**A:** No. CONATEL certification is required for telecommunications equipment (radios, base stations). Our solution is software-only for feedback analysis, not telecommunications equipment. No regulatory approval needed.

**Q4: "What happens if there's a data breach?"**
**A:** Risk is minimal because we don't store customer PII and implement 24-hour automatic deletion. In the unlikely event of unauthorized access, there's no persistent customer data to breach. Additionally, we provide full audit logs for any investigation.

**Q5: "Can you provide a Data Processing Agreement?"**
**A:** Yes, we'll provide a DPA aligned with GDPR and Paraguay's new Personal Data Protection Law, clearly defining roles, responsibilities, and data handling procedures.

**Q6: "How does AI decision-making work? Are there automated decisions affecting customers?"**
**A:** Our AI provides analytical insights (emotions, pain points, churn risk scores), not automated decisions. Personal Paraguay's team makes all decisions based on these insights. This aligns with the new law's protections against solely automated decision-making.
