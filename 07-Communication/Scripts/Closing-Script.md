## Overview

This script guides the final 5-10 minutes of the negotiation meeting when it's time to close and commit to next steps. Use this after the demo, value proposition, and pricing discussion have been completed.

**Key Principle:** Close on next steps, not just on "yes/no." Even if they need approval, you want commitment to the process and timeline.

---

## Closing Strategy

### The Assumptive Close
We'll use an **assumptive close** approach - act as if they're moving forward, and discuss next steps naturally. This is appropriate given:
- ✅ 2-month engagement (trust built)
- ✅ Demo completed and well-received
- ✅ Clear pain point and value demonstrated
- ✅ José Luis is a champion (wants this to work)

### Three Possible Outcomes

**Outcome 1: Immediate Commitment** 🎯
- They say yes and have authority to proceed
- Rare, but possible (especially Pilot or Small tier)
- Move directly to contract and next steps

**Outcome 2: Needs Approval** 🟡 (Most Likely)
- They're interested but need CFO/CTO/Exec approval
- Get commitment to internal advocacy and timeline
- Provide materials for stakeholders

**Outcome 3: Not Ready** 🧊
- Objections, concerns, or truly not interested
- Uncover real reason, address if possible
- Set follow-up or graceful exit

**Purpose:**

- Confirm all concerns addressed
- Transition naturally to closing

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "No questions, this looks great" → Move to STEP 2 immediately
- ⚠️ "I'm wondering about X" → Address objection, then STEP 2
- 🧊 "I need to think about it" → Use "Think About It" objection script, then reassess

### STEP 2: Assumptive Close (Propose Next Steps)

**Kyrian:**
```
"Great. So here's what I'd suggest as next steps:

[PAUSE - let them know you're shifting to action]

Based on what you've told me about your needs - the 40 hours a month
of manual work, the need for daily automated reports, the desire to

seems like the best fit.

**1. Internal Alignment (This Week)**
   You'll discuss this with [CFO/CTO/Executive Committee - whoever

   - Executive summary (1-page ROI overview)
   - Technical specifications
   - Implementation timeline
   - Security documentation
   - Contract template for legal review

   What else would be helpful for that conversation?

**2. Follow-Up Meeting (Next Week)**
   We schedule 30 minutes to answer any questions that come up from
   your internal review. Would you like us to join that internal
   meeting, or would you prefer to present it yourself?

**3. Contract & Kick-Off (End of Month)**

```

**Purpose:**
- Assume they're moving forward (psychological commitment)
- Make it EASY (clear steps, support provided)
- Get commitment to timeline
- Identify approval process

**Watch for:**
- ✅ "Yes, that works" → Continue to STEP 3
- ⚠️ "I need more time" → Ask "What would you need to see to feel ready by [DATE]?"
- ⚠️ "That's too fast" → Ask "What timeline would work better?" (uncover real issue)

**Purpose:**

- ✅ Send calendar invite immediately after call (while top of mind)
- ✅ Offer to be available between now and then

**DON'T:**
- ❌ Say "let me know when works for you" (puts burden on them)
- ❌ Wait for them to suggest next step (you lead the process)

**DO NOT immediately discount.** Defend value first.

**Option 2: Smaller Tier**
- Start with Small tier (40M PYG) instead of Medium (80M PYG)

**Purpose:**
- Defend value (don't cheapen product)
- Offer structural alternatives (not discounts)

**ONLY discount if:**
- They commit to longer term (e.g., 20% off for 12-month commitment)
- They agree to be case study / testimonial

**Never discount without getting something in return.**

## Key Principles - Memorize These

### 1. Always Be Closing (ABC)
Don't wait until the end - close throughout the meeting:
- After demo: "Does this solve your automation need?"
- After ROI: "Does this justify the investment?"
- After pricing: "Which tier fits your needs best?"

### 2. Trial Close Early
Gauge readiness before the final close:
- "How are you feeling about this so far?"
- "Does this make sense for your situation?"
- "What questions do you have?"

### 3. Assumptive Language
Act like they're moving forward:
- "When we get started..." (not "if")
- "Here's what happens next..." (not "here's what would happen")
- "After you're live..." (not "if you decide to move forward")

### 4. Make It Easy
Remove friction from saying yes:
- Provide all materials proactively
- Offer to support internal approval
- Give clear next steps
- Make timeline realistic and achievable

### 5. Ask for the Commitment
Don't be afraid to directly ask:
- "Are you ready to move forward?"
- "Can we get this started?"
- "Do you want to proceed with [TIER]?"

### 6. Silence is Powerful
After asking for commitment, SHUT UP and wait:
- First person to speak loses
- Let them process
- Don't fill silence with more talking

### 7. Handle Objections Early
Surface and address concerns before closing:
- "What questions do you still have?"
- "Is there anything holding you back?"
- Better to know objections NOW than later

### 8. Create Urgency (But Don't Pressure)
Give a reason to act now (without being pushy):
- "We can have you live by early November if we start by end of month"
- "The sooner you start, the sooner you save those 40 hours/month"
- NOT: "This price is only available today" (feels salesy)

### 9. Win the Next Step (Not Always the Deal)
If you can't close the deal today, close on the next step:
- Schedule next meeting
- Get commitment to internal presentation
- Agree to pilot program
- SOMETHING to maintain momentum

### 10. Graceful Exit if Needed
Know when to walk away professionally:
- Don't beg or pressure
- Leave door open for future
- Request feedback to learn
- Preserve relationship
