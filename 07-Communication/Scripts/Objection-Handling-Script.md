## How to Use This Script

**When objection arises:**
1. **Listen fully** - don't interrupt
2. **Acknowledge** - "I understand your concern..."
3. **Clarify** - "Just to make sure I understand, you're concerned about [X]?"
4. **Respond** - Use appropriate script below
5. **Confirm** - "Does that address your concern?"

**Remember:**
- Stay calm and confident
- Use data, not emotion
- Never get defensive
- Ask clarifying questions

## Objection #1: "That's Too Expensive"

### Version A: ROI Focus (Primary Response)

**Script:**
> "I understand - let's look at the investment in context.
>
> You're currently spending $24,000 per year just on the labor for manual analysis - that's the direct cost. But the total cost of the current manual process is actually around $364,000 per year when you include:
> - Missed churn signals (customers leaving you could have saved)
> - Wrong product decisions (building features nobody wants)
> - Analyst time (that could be spent on strategic work)
>
> Our solution costs $6,098 in Year 1, and saves you $357,902. That's a payback period of less than one week.
>
> So the question isn't whether it's expensive - it's whether you want to keep losing $364,000 a year, or invest $6,098 once to stop that bleeding.
>
> Would you like to see the detailed ROI calculator?"

**When to use:** Default response for price objection

**Key points:**
- Reframe from "cost" to "investment"
- Show massive ROI (5,873%)
- Compare to current pain cost ($364k/year)
- Offer calculator for proof

---

### Version B: Comparison Wedge (Alternative Response)

**Script:**
> "I understand. Let's compare your options:
>
> **Enterprise SaaS** (Qualtrics, Medallia):
> - Cost: $80,000 over 3 years
> - Timeline: 3-6 months implementation
> - Result: Vendor lock-in, no source code
>
> **Build in-house:**
> - Cost: $135,000 in development + $364,000 lost during that year = $499,000 total
> - Timeline: 12 months
> - Risk: May not work, ongoing maintenance burden
>
> **Status quo** (continue manual):
> - Cost: $364,000 per year, forever
> - Result: Problem gets worse as volume grows
>
> **Our solution:**
> - Cost: $5,714 (one-time) + $384/year (infrastructure)
> - Timeline: 2 weeks
> - Result: Source code included, zero vendor lock-in
>
> We're 90-98% cheaper than every alternative, and 26 times faster. Which of these options makes the most sense for Personal Paraguay?"

**When to use:** If they're comparing to alternatives

**Key points:**
- Side-by-side comparison shows we're obviously best value
- Emphasize speed advantage (2 weeks vs. 3-12 months)
- Highlight source code (zero lock-in)
- Make them choose (forces decision)

### Version C: Pilot Option (If Budget is Truly Tight)

**Script:**
> "I hear you - budget is a real concern. What if we did this differently?
>
> Instead of the full package right now, let's start with a 10-day pilot:
> - You pay 20-30 million PYG
> - We analyze one dataset with your real data (up to 3,000 comments)
> - You get the full dashboard, Excel export, everything
> - If it delivers the value we're showing, we credit that toward the full package
> - If not, you've only invested $2,857-$4,286 to see proof
>
> This way, you see results with your own data in 10 days, with minimal risk. And if it works (which we know it will), you upgrade to the full tier and we credit the pilot fee.
>
> Would that make more sense for your budget?"

**When to use:** If they have genuine budget constraints

**Key points:**
- Lowers entry barrier
- Proof before full commitment
- Credit pilot fee (not wasted money)
- 10 days to results (fast proof)

> - Maintenance and bug fixes (ongoing developer time)
> - Infrastructure management
> - Future enhancements
>

> - May not achieve our 87% AI cost savings innovation (our hybrid architecture took R&D)

**When to use:** Default response to in-house objection

## Objection #4: "We Need to Think About It / Get Back to You"

### Version A: Uncover Real Concern (Primary Response)

**Script:**
> "Of course, take the time you need. This is an important decision.
>
> Before you go, can I ask - what specific concerns or questions do you still have? I want to make sure we've addressed everything so you can make an informed decision.
>
> Is it:
> - Technical fit? (Does it solve your problem?)
> - Pricing? (Does it fit your budget?)
> - Approval process? (Do you need materials for stakeholders?)
> - Timing? (When can we deploy?)
> - Risk? (First client concerns?)
> - Something else?
>
> I'd rather address any concerns now while we're together, rather than leave you with unanswered questions."

**Then:**
- Listen carefully to real concern
- Address it specifically
- Ask: "If we resolve that, is there anything else preventing us from moving forward?"

**When to use:** Always, when they want to "think about it"

**Key points:**
- Uncover real objection (not stalling)
- Address specific concern
- Set clear next steps
- Don't leave with vague "think about it"

## General Objection Handling Principles

### DO:
- ✅ Listen fully before responding
- ✅ Acknowledge their concern ("I understand...")
- ✅ Use data and facts (not emotion)
- ✅ Ask clarifying questions
- ✅ Reframe to value (not just cost)
- ✅ Offer alternatives (pilot, tiers, payment terms)
- ✅ Stay calm and confident
- ✅ Confirm objection is addressed

### DON'T:
- ❌ Interrupt them
- ❌ Get defensive
- ❌ Argue or dismiss concern
- ❌ Make it emotional
- ❌ Badmouth competitors
- ❌ Cave immediately on price
- ❌ Show desperation

## Walk-Away Objections

### When to Walk Away

If they insist on:
1. **Below 15M PYG** - Below cost, hard pass
2. **Unlimited scope/support** - Unsustainable
3. **IP ownership before payment** - Protect IP
4. **24/7 support** - Impossible for 3-person team
5. **Unreasonable demands** - Respect yourself

### How to Walk Away Gracefully

**Script:**
> "José Luis, I appreciate the time you've invested in evaluating our solution over these past 2 months. Unfortunately, I don't think we can find common ground that works for both sides.
>
> Our minimum to deliver a quality solution that we can stand behind is 40 million PYG. I understand that doesn't fit your current budget/requirements.
>
> If circumstances change in the future, or if you'd like to revisit this conversation, please feel free to reach out. I wish you the best in finding a solution that works for Personal Paraguay.
>
> Thank you for considering us."

**Then:**
- Stand up (if in person)
- Shake hands professionally
- Leave with dignity
- Don't burn the bridge (they may come back)

**Remember: You have leverage. They need us more than we need them. Don't be afraid to walk away.**
