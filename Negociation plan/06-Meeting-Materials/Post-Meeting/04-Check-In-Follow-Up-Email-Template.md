## Follow-Up Strategy & Timeline

### Day 0: Send Proposal
- Send comprehensive proposal package
- Include all requested materials

### Day 3-5: First Follow-Up (Soft)
- Quick check-in
- Offer to answer questions
- No pressure tone
- Keep it brief

### Day 7-10: Second Follow-Up (Direct)
- More specific questions
- Offer to present to stakeholders
- Ask about timeline
- Still respectful, slightly more direct

### Day 14-21: Final Follow-Up (Close Loop)
- Give them an out
- Ask them to confirm status
- Promise not to follow up again if no response
- Leave door open for future

### After Day 21: Move On
- If no response, consider dead
- Focus energy elsewhere
- But remain friendly if they reach out later

## When NOT to Send Follow-Up

**Don't follow up if:**
- They explicitly said "We'll contact you by [DATE]" and that date hasn't passed
- They're in a known internal review process with specific timeline
- They asked you not to follow up
- You have a scheduled call already

**Do follow up if:**
- Radio silence with no explanation
- Past expected decision date
- They said they'd get back to you by X date and didn't
- You sense they're stuck and need help

## Variations by Situation

### If They Mentioned Budget Approval Process:
Add:
> "I know you mentioned the budget approval typically takes [X weeks]. Have you been able to start that process? Is there anything we can provide to help support the business case?"

### If They Mentioned Specific Stakeholder Concerns:
Add:
> "I remember you mentioned your CTO had questions about [technical topic]. Did the technical brief we provided address those concerns, or should Jonathan schedule a call with your CTO to discuss further?"

### If Competitor Mentioned:
Add:
> "I know you were also evaluating [Competitor]. Have you made a decision, or are you still in the comparison process? We're happy to provide additional information if helpful."

### If They Seemed Very Interested:
Adjust tone to be warmer:
> "I thought our meeting went really well, and you seemed excited about the automation potential. I'm curious what happened - did something change, or is this just taking longer than expected on your end?"

## Response Scenarios & How to Handle

### Response: "Still reviewing internally"
**Your Reply:**
> "Great! No rush. Is there anything we can provide to help with the internal review? What timeline are you working toward?"

### Response: "Budget got frozen"
**Your Reply:**
> "I understand - budget constraints happen. When do you expect budget to free up? We can revisit then. In the meantime, would a lower-cost pilot make sense, or should we just reconnect later?"

### Response: "Went with another solution"
**Your Reply:**
> "Thanks for letting me know. I appreciate you closing the loop. Out of curiosity, what made you choose the other solution? (Helps us improve for future clients.) And if it doesn't work out, we're here if you want to revisit."

### Response: "Not interested anymore"
**Your Reply:**
> "I understand - thanks for being direct. Can I ask what changed from our meeting when you seemed interested? Just trying to learn and improve for future clients."

### Response: "Can you lower the price?"
**Your Reply:**
> "I'd like to understand what budget you have available. Our pricing is based on value delivered ($350k+ saved), but we can discuss the pilot option at [lower price] to prove value first, or adjust scope to fit your budget. What number were you thinking?"

## Tone Guidelines

**First Follow-Up:**
- Light and friendly
- No pressure
- Offer to help
- Keep it brief

**Second Follow-Up:**
- More direct
- Ask specific questions
- Still respectful
- Offer solutions

**Final Follow-Up:**
- Give them an out
- Be honest
- Promise not to bug them
- Leave door open

**Always:**
- Professional
- Respectful
- Value their time
- Don't be desperate
